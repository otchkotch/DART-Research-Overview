01 / MISSION

A protective function, not a parts list.

Evaluate useful warning, source bearing and total lifetime cost together. A cheaper presence detector is not automatically equivalent to a directional station.

Operating concept — under investigation

Independent listening stations

Each unwired station would acquire sound, identify credible drone events and transmit concise observations to a shared gateway. Renewable power, local processing, weather protection and serviceability belong in the same engineering model.

The raised acoustic head, PV collectors, electronics body and stored energy remain a conceptual assembly. Panel count, exact components and deployment topology are unapproved.

Capability contract

Presence, bearing and motion differ

Presence indicates a likely drone sound. Bearing points toward its acoustic source. Motion requires time-consistent observations and association. A first-alert station location does not supply an independent source bearing.

Actual deployment conditions, target classes, nuisance-alert tolerance and useful angular uncertainty must be defined before a design can win.

What the calculations establish

Derive precision from useful direction.

Increasing acoustic spacing from 11 to 33 mm gives three times the ideal timing sensitivity with the same four channels. It does not prove three times the range or field accuracy.

An illustrative 5° total angular RMS budget permits 1.40 µs per-channel timing uncertainty at 11 mm, versus 4.20 µs at 33 mm, if the entire budget is spent on independent timing errors.

Geometry, port response, pose, wind and other noise consume that same budget. RMS is not a worst-case tolerance. Sub-sample delay estimation is possible; sample rate alone does not set accuracy.

03 / EVIDENCE

Promising field results. Specific transfer limits.

Acoustic drone detection and bearings have been demonstrated elsewhere. Their source, receiver, geometry, processing and conditions must accompany every distance claim.

Primary sourceWhat it supportsWhat it does not establish
ARL · 2014 ↗Four outdoor flights · much larger tetrahedral apparatusReported 800–1,700 Hz ROC: 99.5% detection below 450 m at 0.3% false-positive probability; 99% at 450–600 m at 3%.A DART detection radius, multi-season reliability or false alerts/hour. Other aircraft interfered with tracking.
TU Delft · 2025 ↗Approximately 5 cm planar four-microphone arrayConditional outdoor bearings with <10° reported error at 80/100 m for two drones, using 2–5 kHz and 45° array mounting.The same precision for the 11 mm DART aperture, every direction or different targets and backgrounds.
Jung et al. · ICA 2022Ten microphones · 80 mm spacing · anechoic experimentCombined intensity modules reported 4.6° azimuth / 3.8° elevation RMSE for the tested white-noise sources.Four-microphone outdoor drone performance or a universal manufacturing tolerance.
No verified DART operating radius exists. Recoverable target spectrum, background masking, wind, propagation, processing dwell and source separation govern detection. Source-bearing quality may become useful later than presence detection.
How to read the evidence

External measurements apply to the cited experiment. Calculations expose consequences of stated assumptions. Manufacturer specifications describe components. Supplier prices are dated offers, not complete assemblies. DART validation requires its own documented system measurements.

Sound propagation also consumes warning time. For constant-speed approach, warning after delivery is x/v − r/c − T, where x is along-path distance to the boundary at emission, r is distance to the listening station, and T includes dwell, processing and communication.

Installation, gateway, R&D and qualified service life remain unresolved. Some prototype parts are not suitable for unattended deployment. No defensible finished-node minimum price is established.

Strongest cost opportunities
  • Reduce manufacturing operations.Fewer harness connections, separate boards, seals and calibration steps—without losing the four-channel function.
  • Reduce continuous processing energy.Lower load may reduce solar and storage requirements if weak-event sensitivity and warning latency survive.
  • Quote the complete acoustic assembly.Four high-SNR capsules are about $6 at the cited 400-piece tiers; that excludes substrates, weather ports, assembly and test.
  • Count field costs and failures.A cheaper station can cost more overall if it needs more placements, gateways or service visits.
Current price observations and cost interpretation

Observed 29 October 2026, US/USD: four older high-SNR IM72D128V01XTMA1 capsules at a 400-piece order cost approximately $6.12/node; four newer IM72D128VV01XTMA1 capsules approximately $5.98, with zero stock displayed at the checked supplier. The lower-SNR IM66D130MV01XTMA1 comparator is approximately $2.98 for four. Saving about $3.14 does not establish equivalent weak-signal performance.

05 / DECISIONS

Resolve what could change the design.

Use existing physics and published evidence first. A physical test earns its place when its result could reverse a consequential engineering decision.

01

Define useful information.

Specify target conditions, desired warning probability, false alerts/hour and bearing uncertainty. Sixty seconds alone does not specify sensor range or topology.

02

Compare assembly and full costs.

Request functionally comparable four-channel head/PCBA, safe power and assembly quotations, including test/yield and recurring service. No manufacturing winner is selected.

03

Check finished acoustic paths.

Use one reconfigurable synchronous instrument to compare aperture, port stability and relevant wind/wet conditions. Stable bias may be calibrated; changing port response may not.

04

Measure the joint outcome.

Evaluate weak-event detection, first useful bearing, latency and continuous energy together. A short screening trial is not rare-false-alarm or all-season qualification.
